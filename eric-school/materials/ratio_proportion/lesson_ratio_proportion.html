Ratio & Proportion

Maths 60 minutes
01

Simplifying ratios

10 min

A ratio compares two or more quantities. It's written with a colon, e.g. 3:5.

To simplify a ratio, divide every part by the same number — their highest common factor — just like simplifying a fraction.

15:20  →  divide both by 5  →  3:4

If the quantities have different units, convert to the same unit first, then simplify.

2 kg : 500 g  →  2000 g : 500 g  →  divide both by 500  →  4:1

A ratio can also be written in the form 1 : n (or n : 1) by dividing both sides by one of the parts — useful for comparing ratios quickly.

8:20  →  divide both by 8  →  1 : 2.5

02

Sharing in a given ratio

12 min

To split an amount in a given ratio:

1. Add up the ratio parts to find the total number of parts.

2. Divide the amount by the total parts to find what one part is worth.

3. Multiply that back up for each share.

Worked example: share £60 in the ratio 2:3:5

Total parts = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10

1 part = £60 ÷ 10 = £6

Shares: 2 × £6 = £12, 3 × £6 = £18, 5 × £6 = £30

Check: £12 + £18 + £30 = £60 ✓

Working backwards: if you're told one share, divide it by its number of parts to find the value of 1 part, then use that to find the total.

Two amounts are shared in the ratio 3:4. The larger share is £48.  →  4 parts = £48  →  1 part = £12  →  total (7 parts) = £84

03

Direct proportion

12 min

Two quantities are in direct proportion when they increase (or decrease) at the same rate — double one, and the other doubles too. This is written y = kx, where k is a fixed number.

Most GCSE direct proportion problems use the unitary method: find the value of 1 unit, then scale up to whatever you need.

5 pens cost £3.50. Find the cost of 8 pens.

1 pen = £3.50 ÷ 5 = £0.70  →  8 pens = 8 × £0.70 = £5.60

A recipe for 4 people needs 200 g of rice. How much for 10 people?

1 person = 200 ÷ 4 = 50 g  →  10 people = 10 × 50 = 500 g

Best buy questions use the same idea — find the price per single item (or per 100g/per litre) for each option, then compare.

Shop A: 6 apples for £2.40 (£0.40 each). Shop B: 10 apples for £3.80 (£0.38 each).  →  Shop B is better value.

04

Inverse proportion

10 min

Two quantities are in inverse proportion when one increases as the other decreases at a matching rate — double one, and the other halves. This is written y = k ÷ x (equivalently xy = k, a constant).

Classic examples: more workers → less time to finish a job; faster speed → less time for a fixed journey.

It takes 4 workers 15 hours to build a wall, all working at the same rate. How long would 6 workers take?

Total "worker-hours" needed = 4 × 15 = 60

6 workers: 60 ÷ 6 = 10 hours

y is inversely proportional to x. When x = 5, y = 12. Find y when x = 3.

k = xy = 5 × 12 = 60  →  y = 60 ÷ 3 = 20

Spotting the difference: if the question says quantities change together (both up or both down), it's direct. If one goes up while the other goes down, it's inverse.

05

GCSE-style review questions

16 min

1. Simplify the ratio 45:18.

[1 mark]
Divide both by 9 → 5:2 (1)

2. Share £84 in the ratio 2:5.

[2 marks]
84 ÷ 7 = £12 (1)   shares = £24 : £60 (1)

3. A shop sells 6 apples for £2.40 and a market stall sells 10 apples for £3.80. Which offers better value? Show your working.

[3 marks]
Shop: 2.40 ÷ 6 = £0.40 per apple (1)   Stall: 3.80 ÷ 10 = £0.38 per apple (1)   Market stall is better value, it's cheaper per apple (1)

4. It takes 4 workers 15 hours to build a wall, all working at the same rate. How long would it take 6 workers?

[3 marks]
Worker-hours = 4 × 15 = 60 (1)   60 ÷ 6 (1)   = 10 hours (1)

5. y is inversely proportional to x. When x = 5, y = 12. Find y when x = 3.

[2 marks]
k = 5 × 12 = 60 (1)   y = 60 ÷ 3 = 20 (1)

Tip: read the question carefully to decide direct or inverse before you start — "the more/faster ... the less/fewer" is your inverse-proportion signal.
