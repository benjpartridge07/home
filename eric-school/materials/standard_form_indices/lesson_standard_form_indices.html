Laws of indices

12 min

An index (or power) tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself. In 34, 3 is the base and 4 is the index.

  • Multiplying (same base): am × an = am+n — add the powers
  • Dividing (same base): am ÷ an = am−n — subtract the powers
  • Power of a power: (am)n = am×n — multiply the powers

Worked examples: 34 × 32 = 36.   57 ÷ 53 = 54.   (23)2 = 26 = 64.

These laws only work when the base is the same on both sides — you can't combine 34 × 22 this way.

02

Zero and negative indices

10 min

Anything to the power 0 is 1: a0 = 1 (as long as a ≠ 0). e.g. 90 = 1, 1000 = 1.

A negative index means "reciprocal": a−n = 1 ÷ an. The negative sign does not make the answer negative — it flips it into a fraction.

Worked examples: 2−1 = 1÷2 = 0.5.   5−2 = 1÷52 = 1÷25 = 1/25.   10−3 = 1÷1000 = 0.001.

03

Standard form: writing large and small numbers

12 min

Standard form writes any number as A × 10n, where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer (positive, negative, or zero).

  • Large numbers (10 or more) use a positive power — n counts how many places the decimal point moves left.
  • Small numbers (less than 1) use a negative power — n counts how many places the decimal point moves right.

Worked examples: 5,700,000 = 5.7 × 106.   0.0032 = 3.2 × 10−3.

04

Calculating with standard form

12 min

To multiply: multiply the two front numbers, and add the powers of 10.

(A × 10m) × (B × 10n) = (A × B) × 10m+n

To divide: divide the front numbers, and subtract the powers of 10.

(A × 10m) ÷ (B × 10n) = (A ÷ B) × 10m−n

Worked example: (3 × 104) × (2 × 103) = 6 × 107 (already in standard form — done).

Watch out: if the front numbers multiply to 10 or more, it's no longer in standard form — adjust it. e.g. (5 × 106) × (4 × 103) = 20 × 109 = 2 × 1010 (moved the point, so the power went up by 1).

Worked example: (8 × 107) ÷ (2 × 103) = 4 × 104.
