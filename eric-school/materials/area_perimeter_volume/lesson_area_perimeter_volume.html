Area, Perimeter & Volume

Maths 84 minutes
01

Area & perimeter: rectangles, triangles & parallelograms

10 min

Rectangle: area = length × width. Perimeter = 2(length + width). Example: 8 cm by 5 cm → area = 40 cm², perimeter = 2(8+5) = 26 cm.

Triangle: area = ½ × base × height, using the perpendicular (vertical) height, not a slanted side. Example: base 10 cm, height 6 cm → area = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm².

Parallelogram: area = base × perpendicular height (it's a rectangle "pushed over" — same area). Example: base 12 cm, height 7 cm → area = 12 × 7 = 84 cm².

Perimeter of any straight-sided shape is just the total distance around the outside — add up all the side lengths.

02

Area of a trapezium

9 min

A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides, of lengths a and b, separated by perpendicular height h.

Area = ½ × (a + b) × h — add the two parallel sides, halve the total, then multiply by the height.

Worked example: parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, height 4 cm → area = ½ × (6+10) × 4 = ½ × 16 × 4 = 32 cm².

03

Circles: area & circumference

12 min

Radius (r) is the distance from the centre to the edge. Diameter (d) goes all the way across through the centre — d = 2r.

Area = πr² — square the radius, then multiply by π. Worked example: r = 5 cm → area = π × 5² = π × 25 = 78.5 cm² (3 s.f., using π ≈ 3.14).

Circumference = πd = 2πr — the distance all the way around. Worked example: d = 12 cm → circumference = π × 12 = 37.7 cm (3 s.f.).

Exam tip: use the π button on your calculator for full accuracy, and only round your final answer — usually to 1 or 3 significant figures, or leave the answer in terms of π if asked (e.g. 25π cm²).

04

Volume of cuboids & prisms

12 min

Cuboid: volume = length × width × height. Example: 5 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm → volume = 60 cm³.

Prism (a 3D shape with a constant cross-section, like a triangular prism): volume = cross-sectional area × length. First find the area of the end face (the shape you'd see if you sliced straight through), then multiply by how long the prism is.

Worked example (triangular prism): triangular cross-section with base 6 cm, height 4 cm, and the prism is 10 cm long. Cross-sectional area = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm². Volume = 12 × 10 = 120 cm³.

A cuboid is really just a special case of a prism where the cross-section is a rectangle.

05

Volume of a cylinder

10 min

A cylinder is a prism whose cross-section is a circle, so the same rule applies: volume = circular cross-sectional area × height = πr²h.

Worked example: radius 3 cm, height 8 cm → volume = π × 3² × 8 = π × 9 × 8 = π × 72 = 226.2 cm³ (1 d.p.).

Exam tip: watch out for questions that give you the diameter instead of the radius — halve it first before squaring, or you'll get an answer 4 times too big.

06

GCSE-style review questions

16 min

1. A rectangle has length 12 cm and width 7 cm. Find its area and perimeter.

[3 marks]
Area = 12 × 7 = 84 cm² (1)   Perimeter = 2(12+7) (1)   = 38 cm (1)

2. A triangle has a base of 9 cm and a perpendicular height of 6 cm. Calculate its area.

[2 marks]
½ × 9 × 6 (1)   = 27 cm² (1)

3. A trapezium has parallel sides of 8 cm and 14 cm, and a perpendicular height of 5 cm. Calculate its area.

[3 marks]
½ × (8+14) (1)   × 5 (1)   = 55 cm² (1)

4. A circle has a radius of 6 cm. Calculate (a) its area and (b) its circumference, both to 1 decimal place.

[4 marks]
(a) π × 6² (1)   = 113.1 cm² (1)   (b) π × 12 (1)   = 37.7 cm (1)

5. A semicircular flower bed has a diameter of 10 m. Calculate the perimeter of the flower bed (the curved edge plus the straight edge), to 1 decimal place.

[3 marks]
Curved part = ½ × π × 10 = 15.7 m (1)   Straight edge = 10 m (1)   Total = 15.7 + 10 = 25.7 m (1)

6. A cuboid has length 8 cm, width 5 cm and height 4 cm. Calculate its volume.

[2 marks]
8 × 5 × 4 (1)   = 160 cm³ (1)

7. A triangular prism has a cross-section with base 10 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm, and the prism is 15 cm long. Calculate its volume.

[3 marks]
Cross-sectional area = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm² (1)   Volume = 30 × 15 (1)   = 450 cm³ (1)

8. A cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 12 cm. Calculate its volume to 1 decimal place.

[3 marks]
π × 4² × 12 (1)   = π × 192 (1)   = 603.2 cm³ (1)

Tip: for triangles, parallelograms and trapeziums, always use the perpendicular height, never a slanted side. For circles, remember d = 2r — if you're given the diameter, halve it before squaring for the area. For any prism (including cylinders), volume = cross-sectional area × length/height.
