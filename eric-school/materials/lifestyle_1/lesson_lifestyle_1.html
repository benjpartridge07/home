Disease risk & cardiovascular health

Biology 50 minutes
01

Communicable vs non-communicable disease

8 min

A communicable disease is caused by a pathogen (bacterium, virus, fungus or protist) and can spread from person to person.

A non-communicable disease cannot be spread between people. It develops because of a combination of genetic, lifestyle and environmental factors, often building up over a long period of time. Examples: cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, some cancers, chronic lung disease.

02

Risk factors for non-communicable disease

12 min

A risk factor is something that increases the probability of developing a disease — it does not guarantee that someone will get it.

Risk factorLinked to
SmokingLung disease, lung cancer, cardiovascular disease
Poor diet (high fat/salt/sugar)Obesity, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease
Excess alcoholLiver disease, some cancers
Lack of exerciseObesity, cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes
ObesityType 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, some cancers

Exam tip — correlation vs cause

A study can show a correlation between a factor and a disease (they rise and fall together), but that alone doesn't prove the factor causes the disease — there could be another linked variable. Scientists look for a plausible mechanism and repeat studies before accepting a causal link.

03

Cardiovascular disease & its treatments

14 min

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is disease of the heart or blood vessels. In coronary heart disease, fatty deposits (atheroma) build up inside the coronary arteries, narrowing them and restricting blood flow to the heart muscle — this can starve heart tissue of oxygen.

TreatmentWhat it does
StatinsDrugs that reduce blood cholesterol level, slowing the build-up of fatty deposits
StentsTiny mesh tubes inserted into a narrowed artery to hold it open and restore blood flow
Coronary bypass surgeryA healthy blood vessel from elsewhere in the body is used to bypass the blocked section of a coronary artery
Artificial hearts / valvesMechanical devices that pump blood or replace a faulty valve, e.g. while a patient waits for a transplant
Heart transplantA severely diseased heart is replaced with a healthy donor heart

Exam tip — evaluate, don't just describe

"Evaluate" questions want pros and cons. E.g. statins avoid surgery but must be taken long-term and can cause side effects; surgery (bypass/transplant) can fix the problem more directly but carries the risks of any major operation, and donor hearts are in short supply.

04

Homeostasis — the big idea

8 min

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a constant internal environment, despite changes inside or outside the body. The body constantly monitors and controls things like body temperature, blood glucose concentration, and water content.

The general pattern: a receptor detects a change away from the normal level, and a coordination system (nerves or hormones) triggers an effector to respond and bring the level back to normal.

  • Homeostasis matters because cells (and the enzymes inside them) only work properly within a narrow range of conditions
  • This lesson only needs the big picture — the detail of how blood glucose is controlled by insulin is covered next, in Lifestyle & Health, Part 2: Hormones

05

GCSE-style review questions

8 min

1. What is a non-communicable disease?

[2 marks]
A disease that cannot be spread between people (1); caused by a combination of genetic, lifestyle and/or environmental factors (1).

2. State two lifestyle risk factors linked to cardiovascular disease.

[2 marks]
Any two: smoking (1); poor diet / high fat diet (1); lack of exercise (1); obesity (1); excess alcohol (1).

3. A newspaper reports that "people who drink a certain energy drink are more likely to develop heart disease." Explain why this does not prove the drink causes heart disease.

[2 marks]
This only shows a correlation between the two things (1); there could be another linked factor causing both, e.g. people who drink it also have a poor diet/don't exercise, so a direct cause hasn't been shown (1).

4. Describe how a stent is used to treat coronary heart disease.

[2 marks]
A stent (mesh tube) is inserted into the narrowed coronary artery (1) and holds it open, restoring blood flow to the heart muscle (1).

5. Evaluate the use of statins compared with coronary bypass surgery to treat someone at risk of a heart attack.

[4 marks]
Statins: don't require surgery, lower risk in the short term (1); but must be taken long-term and can cause side effects (1). Bypass surgery: can restore blood flow more directly/permanently (1); but carries the risks of major surgery and a longer recovery time (1).

6. What is meant by homeostasis? Give one example of something the body keeps constant.

[2 marks]
The maintenance of a constant internal environment despite changes inside or outside the body (1); e.g. body temperature / blood glucose concentration / water content (1).

Tip: if a question says "risk factor," it never means "cause" — it means something that makes a disease more likely. Keep that word choice ("increases the chance/likelihood of") in your answers.
