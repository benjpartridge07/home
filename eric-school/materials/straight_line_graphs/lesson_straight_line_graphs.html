Straight-Line Graphs

Maths 60 minutes
01

y = mx + c

12 min

Every straight line can be written as y = mx + c, where:

  • m is the gradient — how steep the line is (and which way it slopes)
  • c is the y-intercept — the value of y where the line crosses the y-axis (when x = 0)

A bigger gradient means a steeper line. A negative gradient means the line slopes downward as you move left to right.

gradient = rise ÷ run = 2 ÷ 1 = 2  ·  y-intercept = 1 (line crosses the y-axis at (0, 1))

02

Finding the gradient from two points

10 min

If you know two points a line passes through, use: m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁) — the change in y divided by the change in x.

Worked example: find the gradient through (1, 3) and (4, 9).

m = (9 − 3) ÷ (4 − 1) = 6 ÷ 3 = 2

Another: find the gradient through (−2, 5) and (2, −3).

m = (−3 − 5) ÷ (2 − (−2)) = −8 ÷ 4 = −2

03

Plotting from a table of values, and parallel lines

12 min

To plot a line, pick a few x-values, work out the matching y-values, plot the points, then join them with a straight line (and extend it across the grid).

x−1012
y = 2x + 1−1135

Parallel lines have the same gradient (but different y-intercepts, so they never meet). e.g. y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x − 3 are parallel — both have gradient 2.

y = 2x + 1 and y = 3x + 1 are not parallel, even though they share the same y-intercept — their gradients (2 and 3) are different.

04

Finding the equation of a line

12 min

Given a table (or a graph), work out m from how much y changes each time x increases by 1, and read c from the y-value when x = 0.

Worked example:

x0123
y471013

y increases by 3 every time x increases by 1  →  gradient m = 3. When x = 0, y = 4  →  y-intercept c = 4.

Equation: y = 3x + 4

05

GCSE-style review questions

14 min

1. State the gradient and y-intercept of y = 4x − 7.

[2 marks]
Gradient = 4 (1)   y-intercept = −7 (1)

2. Find the gradient of the line joining (2, 1) and (6, 9).

[2 marks]
(9 − 1) ÷ (6 − 2) (1)   = 8 ÷ 4 = 2 (1)

3. Is the line y = −3x + 8 parallel to y = −3x + 2? Explain your answer.

[2 marks]
Both lines have gradient −3 (1)   so yes, they are parallel (1)

4. A straight line passes through (0, 5) and has gradient 2. Write down its equation.

[2 marks]
m = 2, c = 5 (1)   = y = 2x + 5 (1)

5. Complete the table for y = 2x − 1 when x = 0, 1, 2, 3, and state the gradient.

[3 marks]
y-values: −1, 1, 3, 5 (1 for all correct)   y increases by 2 each time (1)   gradient = 2 (1)

Tip: to check two points really are on the same line, work out the gradient between each pair — if all the gradients match, the points are collinear.
