Expanding, Factorising & Solving Equations

Maths 65 minutes
01

Expanding single brackets

10 min

Expanding a bracket means multiplying everything inside it by whatever is outside: a(b + c) = ab + ac.

Watch the sign carefully when the number outside is negative — it flips the sign of everything inside.

Worked examples: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12.   5(2x − 3) = 10x − 15.   −2(x + 6) = −2x − 12.   −(3x − 5) = −3x + 5.

02

Expanding double brackets

12 min

Two brackets multiplied together need every term in the first multiplied by every term in the second — often remembered as FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last.

Worked example: (x + 3)(x + 5)

First: x×x = x²  ·  Outer: x×5 = 5x  ·  Inner: 3×x = 3x  ·  Last: 3×5 = 15

= x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15

BracketsExpanded & simplified
(x + 2)(x + 6)x² + 8x + 12
(x − 2)(x + 7)x² + 5x − 14
(x − 4)(x − 3)x² − 7x + 12
03

Factorising

14 min

Factorising is the reverse of expanding — writing an expression as brackets multiplied together.

Common factor: take out the highest common factor (HCF) of every term.

6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)  ·  10x² − 15x = 5x(2x − 3)

Factorising a quadratic x² + bx + c: find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.

x² + 7x + 12 — need two numbers that multiply to 12, add to 7 → 3 and 4 → (x + 3)(x + 4)

x² + 2x − 15 — need two numbers that multiply to −15, add to 2 → 5 and −3 → (x + 5)(x − 3)

04

Solving linear equations

14 min

Use the balance method: whatever you do to one side, do exactly the same to the other side, until x is on its own.

Worked example: 3x + 7 = 22

−7 both sides: 3x = 15  →  ÷3 both sides: x = 5

With brackets, expand first: 2(x + 3) = 16  →  2x + 6 = 16  →  2x = 10  →  x = 5

With x on both sides, collect the x terms onto one side first: 5x − 3 = 2x + 9  →  3x − 3 = 9  →  3x = 12  →  x = 4

GCSE-style review questions

13 min

1. Expand 4(2x − 3).

[1 mark]
8x − 12 (1)

2. Expand and simplify (x + 4)(x + 2).

[2 marks]
x² + 2x + 4x + 8 (1)   = x² + 6x + 8 (1)

3. Factorise 12x − 8.

[1 mark]
4(3x − 2) (1)

4. Factorise x² + 3x − 10.

[2 marks]
Numbers that multiply to −10, add to 3: 5 and −2 (1)   = (x + 5)(x − 2) (1)

5. Solve 5(x − 2) = 3x + 8.

[3 marks]
5x − 10 = 3x + 8 (1)   2x − 10 = 8 → 2x = 18 (1)   = x = 9 (1)

Tip: always expand brackets fully before you try to simplify or solve — and when factorising a quadratic, check your answer by expanding it back out to make sure you get the original expression.
