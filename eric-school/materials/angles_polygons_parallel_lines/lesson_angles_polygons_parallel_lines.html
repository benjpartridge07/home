Angles, Polygons & Parallel Lines

Maths 84 minutes
01

Angle facts: lines and points

6 min

Angles on a straight line add up to 180°. Example: if one angle is 125°, the other is 180 − 125 = 55°.

Angles around a point add up to 360°. Example: three angles at a point are 90°, 130° and x° — x = 360 − 90 − 130 = 140°.

Vertically opposite angles (formed when two straight lines cross) are equal — they're the pair of angles directly across from each other, like an X shape.

02

Angles in triangles & quadrilaterals

9 min

Angles in a triangle sum to 180°. Example: a triangle has angles 65° and 72° — the third angle = 180 − 65 − 72 = 43°.

In an isosceles triangle, two angles (and two sides) are equal. In an equilateral triangle, all three angles are 60°.

Angles in a quadrilateral sum to 360° (split it into two triangles: 180° + 180°). Example: angles 95°, 88° and 102° — the fourth angle = 360 − 95 − 88 − 102 = 75°.

03

Angles in parallel lines

12 min

Corresponding angles (F-shape) are in matching positions at each intersection — equal. In the diagram: 1&5, 2&6, 3&7, 4&8.

Alternate angles (Z-shape) are between the two parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal — equal. In the diagram: 4&6, 3&5.

Co-interior angles (also called allied angles, C/U-shape) are between the two parallel lines, on the same side of the transversal — they sum to 180°. In the diagram: 4&5, 3&6.

Worked example: if angle 1 = 65°, then angle 3 = 65° (vertically opposite), angle 4 = 115° (straight line with 1), angle 5 = 65° (corresponding to 1), and angle 6 = 115° (co-interior with angle 3, or corresponding to angle 2).

04

Interior & exterior angles of polygons

10 min

Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°, where n is the number of sides. This works because any polygon can be split into (n − 2) triangles.

Worked example: a heptagon has 7 sides — sum = (7 − 2) × 180 = 5 × 180 = 900°.

Exterior angles of any convex polygon always sum to 360° — no matter how many sides. At each vertex, the interior and exterior angle lie on a straight line, so interior + exterior = 180°.

05

Regular polygons

10 min

In a regular polygon, all sides and all angles are equal, so each angle is simply the total shared out evenly.

Each exterior angle = 360° ÷ n  —  Each interior angle = 180° − exterior angle

Worked example (regular pentagon, n = 5): exterior angle = 360 ÷ 5 = 72°; interior angle = 180 − 72 = 108°.

You can also work backwards from an exterior angle to find the number of sides: n = 360 ÷ exterior angle. Example: exterior angle 24° → n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15 sides.

06

GCSE-style review questions

15 min

1. Two angles on a straight line are (2x + 10)° and (3x − 10)°. Find the value of x, then find the size of each angle.

[4 marks]
(2x+10) + (3x−10) = 180 (1)   5x = 180 (1)   x = 36 (1)   angles = 2(36)+10 = 82° and 3(36)−10 = 98° (1)

2. A triangle has angles of 54°, 61° and x°. Find x.

[2 marks]
180 − 54 − 61 (1)   = 65° (1)

3. Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. One angle is 73°. State the size of (a) its corresponding angle, and (b) its co-interior angle, giving a reason for each.

[3 marks]
(a) 73° — corresponding angles are equal (1)   (b) 107° (180 − 73) — co-interior angles sum to 180° (1)   both reasons correctly stated (1)

4. Calculate the sum of the interior angles of a regular nonagon (9 sides).

[2 marks]
(9 − 2) × 180 (1)   = 1260° (1)

5. A regular polygon has an exterior angle of 24°. (a) How many sides does it have? (b) What is the size of each interior angle?

[3 marks]
(a) 360 ÷ 24 = 15 sides (1)   (b) 180 − 24 (1)   = 156° (1)

6. A quadrilateral has angles 85°, 95°, x° and x° (the last two angles are equal). Find x.

[3 marks]
85 + 95 + 2x = 360 (1)   2x = 180 (1)   x = 90° (1)

Tip: for parallel lines, learn the three shapes — F (corresponding, equal), Z (alternate, equal), C/U (co-interior, sum to 180°). For polygons, remember interior sum = (n−2)×180° but exterior angles always sum to 360°, whatever the shape.
