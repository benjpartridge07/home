03

Animal vs plant: what's the difference?

10 min
StructureAnimal cellPlant cell
Nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane✓✓
Mitochondria, ribosomes✓✓
Cell wall (cellulose)—✓
Chloroplasts—✓
Permanent vacuole—✓

Plants can't move around to escape danger or find support, so they need their own built-in structure. The cell wall gives each cell a rigid shape, and millions of rigid cells stacked together is what lets a plant stand upright — think of a stack of rigid boxes versus a pile of soft balloons.

Only cells that carry out photosynthesis (like leaf cells) need chloroplasts — that's why root cells, for example, don't have any.

04

A quick look at bacterial cells

4 min

Bacterial cells are much simpler and smaller than animal or plant cells. Their genetic material isn't held in a nucleus — it's a single loop of DNA floating in the cytoplasm, plus small extra rings of DNA called plasmids.

05

GCSE-style review questions

14 min

1. State the function of the nucleus and the function of the mitochondria.

[2 marks]
Nucleus — contains the genetic material, controls the cell's activities (1). Mitochondria — site of respiration, releases energy (1).

2. Name three structures found in a plant cell that are not found in an animal cell.

[3 marks]
Cell wall (1), chloroplasts (1), permanent vacuole (1).

3. Explain why a root cell (which is underground and never sees light) does not need chloroplasts, but still has a cell wall.

[2 marks]
Chloroplasts are for photosynthesis, which needs light — a root cell gets no light, so it has no use for them (1). A cell wall is needed for structural support/strength, which every plant cell needs regardless of light (1).

4. What is the cell wall made of, and what is its function?

[2 marks]
Cellulose (1). It strengthens and supports the cell (1).

5. State one difference between a bacterial cell and an animal cell, in terms of where the genetic material is found.

[2 marks]
In an animal cell, the genetic material is inside a nucleus (1); in a bacterial cell, it is a single DNA loop (plus plasmids) free in the cytoplasm, not in a nucleus (1).

Tip: "state the function" questions want what the structure does, not just its name or what it's made of — e.g. don't just say "cell wall", say what the cell wall does (strengthens the cell).
