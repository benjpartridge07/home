01

Three ways of saying the same amount

8 min

A fraction, a decimal and a percentage can all describe exactly the same amount — they're just three different ways of writing "part of a whole". Being able to switch between them fast is one of the most-used skills across the whole GCSE course.

Some are worth simply memorising, because they come up constantly:

02

Converting between the three

12 min
  • Fraction → Decimal: divide top by bottom. 3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
  • Decimal → Percentage: multiply by 100 (move the point two places right). 0.75 × 100 = 75%
  • Percentage → Decimal: divide by 100 (move the point two places left). 45% → 0.45
  • Decimal → Fraction: use place value, then simplify. 0.6 = 6/10 = 3/5
  • Fraction → Percentage: convert to a decimal first, then ×100. 9/20 = 0.45 = 45%

03

Ordering FDP values

10 min

When a question mixes fractions, decimals and percentages and asks you to order them, the trick is to convert everything into the same form first — decimals are usually quickest.

Worked example: order 7/20, 30%, 0.28, 1/3 from smallest to largest.

7/20 = 0.35  ·  30% = 0.30  ·  0.28  ·  1/3 = 0.333…

Order: 0.28, 30%, 1/3, 7/20

04

Fraction and percentage of an amount

15 min

Fraction of an amount: divide by the denominator (bottom number), then multiply by the numerator (top number).

3/5 of 40 → 40 ÷ 5 = 8,   8 × 3 = 24

Percentage of an amount, no calculator (building-block method): find 10% by dividing by 10, then 1% by dividing by 10 again, and combine to build any percentage you need.

35% of £60 → 10% = £6, so 30% = £18, and 5% = £3  →  30% + 5% = £21

05

GCSE-style review questions

12 min

1. Convert 7/8 to a decimal.

[1 mark]
0.875 (1)

2. Write 0.34 as a percentage.

[1 mark]
34% (1)

3. Write 65% as a fraction in its simplest form.

[2 marks]
65/100 (1)   simplified = 13/20 (1)

4. Calculate 3/4 of 96.

[2 marks]
96 ÷ 4 = 24 (1)   24 × 3 = 72 (1)

5. Calculate 15% of £220 without a calculator. Show your method.

[3 marks]
10% = £22 (1)   5% = £11 (1)   15% = 22 + 11 = £33 (1)

Tip: whichever form a question gives you, converting to a decimal first is usually the fastest route to compare or order values — just don't forget to convert your final answer back if the question asked for a fraction or percentage.
