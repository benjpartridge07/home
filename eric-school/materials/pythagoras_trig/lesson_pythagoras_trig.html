Pythagoras’ Theorem & Trigonometry

Maths 80 minutes
01

Pythagoras’ theorem: finding the hypotenuse

8 min

Pythagoras’ theorem works for any right-angled triangle. It links the two shorter sides (a and b) to the hypotenuse — the longest side, always opposite the right angle (side c).

a2 + b2 = c2

To find the hypotenuse, add the squares of the two shorter sides, then square root. Worked example: a = 6 cm, b = 8 cm → c2 = 62 + 82 = 36 + 64 = 100 → c = √100 = 10 cm.

02

Pythagoras’ theorem: finding a shorter side

8 min

If you already know the hypotenuse and want a shorter side, rearrange the formula — subtract instead of add:

a2 = c2 − b2    or    b2 = c2 − a2

Worked example: hypotenuse c = 13 cm, one shorter side b = 5 cm → a2 = 132 − 52 = 169 − 25 = 144 → a = √144 = 12 cm.

Common mistake: always check which side is the hypotenuse first (the longest side, opposite the right angle) — it's always c on its own on one side of the equation, never added to another squared side.

03

Introducing SOH CAH TOA

8 min

Right-angled trigonometry links an angle (θ) in a right-angled triangle to the ratio of two of its sides. Relative to the angle θ, the three sides are named the hypotenuse (longest side, opposite the right angle), the opposite (across from θ) and the adjacent (next to θ, not the hypotenuse).

Remember the three ratios with SOH CAH TOA:

SOH: sin θ = opp ÷ hyp    CAH: cos θ = adj ÷ hyp    TOA: tan θ = opp ÷ adj

To choose the right ratio: label the two sides you know (or want) as opp/adj/hyp relative to the angle, then pick the ratio that contains both of them. Make sure your calculator is in degree mode.

04

Trigonometry: finding a missing side

10 min

If you know an angle and one side, and want to find another side, rearrange the ratio to make the unknown side the subject.

Worked example (SOH): angle = 35°, hypotenuse = 9 cm, find the opposite side x. sin 35° = x ÷ 9 → x = 9 × sin 35° = 9 × 0.5736 = 5.2 cm (1 d.p.).

Worked example (CAH): angle = 52°, adjacent = 7 cm, find the hypotenuse h. cos 52° = 7 ÷ h → h = 7 ÷ cos 52° = 7 ÷ 0.6157 = 11.4 cm (1 d.p.).

Tip: if the unknown is on top of the ratio (opp or adj), multiply. If the unknown is on the bottom (hyp, or adj in TOA), you must divide.

05

Trigonometry: finding a missing angle

10 min

If you know two sides and want the angle, use the inverse trig function (sin-1, cos-1 or tan-1 — usually SHIFT + sin/cos/tan on a calculator) to “undo” the ratio.

θ = sin-1(opp ÷ hyp)    θ = cos-1(adj ÷ hyp)    θ = tan-1(opp ÷ adj)

Worked example: opposite = 7 cm, hypotenuse = 11 cm. sin θ = 7 ÷ 11 = 0.6364 → θ = sin-1(0.6364) = 39.5° (1 d.p.).

Worked example: opposite = 8 cm, adjacent = 8 cm. tan θ = 8 ÷ 8 = 1 → θ = tan-1(1) = 45°.

06

GCSE-style review questions

15 min

1. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse.

[2 marks]
c2 = 92 + 122 = 81 + 144 = 225 (1)   c = √225 = 15 cm (1)

2. The hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle is 25 cm and one of the shorter sides is 7 cm. Find the length of the other shorter side.

[3 marks]
a2 = 252 − 72 (1)   = 625 − 49 = 576 (1)   a = √576 = 24 cm (1)

3. In a right-angled triangle, an angle is 28° and the hypotenuse is 14 cm. Find the length of the side opposite the angle, to 1 decimal place.

[3 marks]
sin 28° = x ÷ 14 (1)   x = 14 × sin 28° (1)   = 6.6 cm (1)

4. In a right-angled triangle, the side adjacent to a 50° angle is 9 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse, to 1 decimal place.

[3 marks]
cos 50° = 9 ÷ h (1)   h = 9 ÷ cos 50° (1)   = 14.0 cm (1)

5. A ladder leans against a wall. The foot of the ladder is 2.5 m from the wall and it reaches 6 m up the wall. Calculate the angle the ladder makes with the ground, to 1 decimal place.

[3 marks]
tan θ = 6 ÷ 2.5 = 2.4 (1)   θ = tan-1(2.4) (1)   = 67.4° (1)

6. A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 15 cm and a hypotenuse of 17 cm. (a) Find the angle opposite the 15 cm side, to 1 decimal place. (b) Find the length of the third side.

[4 marks]
(a) sin θ = 15 ÷ 17 (1)   θ = sin-1(15 ÷ 17) = 61.9° (1)   (b) 172 − 152 = 289 − 225 = 64 (1)   √64 = 8 cm (1)

Tip: always find the hypotenuse first — it's the longest side, opposite the right angle. For trigonometry, label opp/adj/hyp relative to the angle you're using, pick SOH, CAH or TOA to match the two sides involved, and check your calculator is in degree mode.
