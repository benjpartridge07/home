Compound Units: Speed, Density & Pressure

Maths 60 minutes
01

What is a compound unit?

6 min

A compound unit is made by dividing (or combining) two other units — e.g. metres per second, grams per cm³, newtons per m². The word “per” is your clue that a compound unit is being used.

All three formulas on this page have exactly the same shape: one quantity ÷ another quantity. Once you can rearrange one of them, you can rearrange all three.

02

Speed = distance ÷ time

10 min

speed = distance ÷ time  —  measured in m/s (metres per second) or km/h (kilometres per hour), depending on the units of distance and time you're given.

Worked example: a runner covers 200 m in 25 s.

speed = 200 ÷ 25 = 8 m/s

Formula triangle: cover the letter you want — cover D and you see S next to T (multiply: D = S × T); cover S and you see D over T (divide: S = D ÷ T); cover T and you see D over S (divide: T = D ÷ S).

Rearranged example: a car travels at 30 m/s for 12 s — distance = speed × time = 30 × 12 = 360 m. A cyclist covers 45 km at 15 km/h — time = distance ÷ speed = 45 ÷ 15 = 3 hours.

03

Density = mass ÷ volume

9 min

density = mass ÷ volume  —  measured in g/cm³ or kg/m³. The same formula triangle idea works: cover mass to multiply (m = ρ × V), cover density or volume to divide.

Worked example: a block has mass 54 g and volume 20 cm³.

density = 54 ÷ 20 = 2.7 g/cm³

Rearranged example: a liquid has density 0.8 g/cm³ and volume 50 cm³ — mass = density × volume = 0.8 × 50 = 40 g.

04

Pressure = force ÷ area

10 min

pressure = force ÷ area  —  measured in N/m², which has its own name: the pascal (Pa). 1 Pa = 1 N/m².

Worked example: a force of 300 N acts on an area of 0.5 m².

pressure = 300 ÷ 0.5 = 600 Pa

For the same force, a smaller area gives a bigger pressure — that's why a sharp knife (small contact area) cuts more easily than a blunt one (large contact area), and why snowshoes (large area) stop you sinking into snow.

05

Converting compound units

12 min

Speed — km/h ↔ m/s: 1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s, so to convert km/h → m/s, ÷ 3.6; to convert m/s → km/h, × 3.6.

72 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s  ·  15 m/s × 3.6 = 54 km/h

Density — g/cm³ ↔ kg/m³: 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³, so to convert g/cm³ → kg/m³, × 1000; kg/m³ → g/cm³, ÷ 1000.

2.7 g/cm³ × 1000 = 2700 kg/m³

Area for pressure — cm² ↔ m²: 1 m² = 10 000 cm² (100 cm × 100 cm), so cm² → m² is ÷ 10 000, and m² → cm² is × 10 000.

ConversionRule
km/h → m/s÷ 3.6
m/s → km/h× 3.6
g/cm³ → kg/m³× 1000
kg/m³ → g/cm³÷ 1000

06

GCSE-style review questions

15 min

1. A cyclist travels 12 km in 40 minutes. Calculate their average speed in km/h, then convert your answer to m/s.

[3 marks]
40 min = 2/3 hour, speed = 12 ÷ (2/3) = 18 km/h (1)   18 ÷ 3.6 (1)   = 5 m/s (1)

2. A block has mass 250 g and volume 100 cm³. Calculate its density in g/cm³, then convert your answer to kg/m³.

[2 marks]
density = 250 ÷ 100 = 2.5 g/cm³ (1)   × 1000 = 2500 kg/m³ (1)

3. A force of 150 N acts on an area of 0.03 m². Calculate the pressure.

[2 marks]
pressure = 150 ÷ 0.03 (1)   = 5000 Pa (1)

4. Explain, using the pressure formula, why a drawing pin has a very sharp point.

[2 marks]
A sharp point has a very small area (1)   for a given force, a smaller area produces a much larger pressure (pressure = force ÷ area), which pushes the pin into the surface more easily (1)

5. A liquid has density 0.9 g/cm³. Calculate the mass of 200 cm³ of this liquid.

[2 marks]
mass = density × volume = 0.9 × 200 (1)   = 180 g (1)

6. A car travels at a constant speed of 90 km/h. Convert this speed to m/s, then calculate how far the car travels in 20 seconds.

[3 marks]
90 ÷ 3.6 (1)   = 25 m/s (1)   distance = 25 × 20 = 500 m (1)

Tip: all three formulas share one shape (quantity ÷ quantity) — learn the "cover it up" triangle trick for speed, and it works for density and pressure too. Always check your units match before converting, and give units in every answer.
