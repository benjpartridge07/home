Plants, Part 2: Photosynthesis

Biology 60 minutes
01

Where photosynthesis happens

10 min

Photosynthesis takes place inside chloroplasts, small organelles found in plant cells (mainly in the leaf). Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, a green pigment that absorbs light energy and uses it to drive the reaction.

Chloroplast

  • Found in plant cells, especially in leaf cells
  • Contains chlorophyll (green pigment)
  • Site of photosynthesis

Palisade mesophyll cell

  • Long, narrow cells near the top of the leaf
  • Packed with chloroplasts — adapted for maximum light absorption
02

The photosynthesis equation

10 min

Photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction — it takes in energy from the environment (light energy) rather than releasing it.

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light energy, absorbed by chlorophyll, in the chloroplasts)
  • Carbon dioxide diffuses into the leaf from the air, through the stomata
  • Water is delivered to the leaf by the xylem (see Plants, Part 1)
  • Glucose can be used for respiration, converted to starch for storage, or used to make cellulose (cell walls) or plant proteins
  • Oxygen is a waste product, released back out through the stomata

03

Factors affecting the rate of photosynthesis

14 min
FactorIncreases the rate when...Why
Light intensityhighermore light energy available to be absorbed by chlorophyll
Carbon dioxide concentrationhighermore CO₂ available as a raw material for the reaction
Temperaturehigher (up to a point)reactions speed up; too hot and enzymes needed for the reaction are denatured, slowing it back down

rate rises, then levels off once another factor becomes limiting

A limiting factor is whichever of the three factors is in shortest supply — it's the one stopping the rate from increasing any further, even if the other two factors increase. On a rate graph, the line levels off (plateaus) at the point where a different factor starts to limit the rate instead.

04

Translocation: moving the glucose around

8 min

Once glucose is made in the leaf, it's often converted to sucrose for transport. Sucrose is moved around the plant — to growing parts, or to storage organs like roots — through the phloem, in both directions. This movement of dissolved sugars through the phloem is called translocation (see Plants, Part 1 for how phloem differs from xylem).

05

Plant nutrition & disease

10 min

As well as glucose from photosynthesis, plants need certain mineral ions from the soil (absorbed by root hair cells) to stay healthy. A shortage of a mineral ion causes a deficiency, which shows up as visible symptoms.

DeficiencySymptomWhy
NitrateStunted growthnitrate is needed to make proteins, for cell growth
MagnesiumYellow leaves (chlorosis)magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll

Plants can also be affected by disease (e.g. caused by fungi, bacteria or viruses) and attack by insects. Signs of plant disease can include: stunted growth, spots on leaves, patches of decay (rot), growths (galls), discolouration, or wilting. Identifying which mineral deficiency or disease is affecting a plant usually means comparing its symptoms against reference charts or images of known diseases and deficiencies.

06

GCSE-style review questions

12 min

1. Name the organelle where photosynthesis takes place, and the pigment it contains that absorbs light.

[2 marks]
Chloroplast (1); chlorophyll (1).

2. Write a word equation for photosynthesis, and state where the energy for the reaction comes from.

[3 marks]
Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (2, allow 1 if one side is wrong/missing); light energy (1).

3. Explain why photosynthesis is described as an endothermic reaction.

[2 marks]
Endothermic reactions take in energy from the surroundings (1); photosynthesis takes in light energy to drive the reaction (1).

4. Palisade mesophyll cells are found near the top of the leaf and are packed with chloroplasts. Explain how this makes them well adapted for photosynthesis.

[2 marks]
Being near the top of the leaf means they receive more light (1); having many chloroplasts means more chlorophyll is available to absorb that light, increasing the rate of photosynthesis (1).

5. A student increases both the light intensity and the CO₂ concentration around a plant, but the rate of photosynthesis stops increasing. Suggest an explanation.

[2 marks]
Another factor (e.g. temperature) has become the limiting factor (1); increasing light and CO₂ further won't raise the rate until that other factor is increased too (1).

6. Explain what happens to the glucose made during photosynthesis, and how it reaches other parts of the plant.

[3 marks]
Used for respiration, converted to starch for storage, or used to make cellulose/proteins (any 1); converted to sucrose for transport (1); moved around the plant through the phloem, by translocation (1).

7. A gardener notices a plant has yellow leaves but is otherwise growing at a normal rate. Suggest which mineral ion it might be short of, and explain why this causes the symptom.

[2 marks]
Magnesium (1); magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll, so a shortage means less chlorophyll (green pigment) in the leaves (1).

Tip: for "explain the rate levels off" questions, always name the specific limiting factor and say why increasing the other two factors can't raise the rate any further.
