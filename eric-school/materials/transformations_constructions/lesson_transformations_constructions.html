Transformations & Constructions

Maths 92 minutes
01

Translation

8 min

A translation slides a shape to a new position — it does not turn it, flip it or change its size. Every point moves the same distance in the same direction.

A translation is described using a column vector: (xy). The top number is the movement left/right (positive = right, negative = left); the bottom number is the movement up/down (positive = up, negative = down).

Worked example: translate point A(1, 4) by the vector (3−2). Add the vector to the coordinates: (1 + 3, 4 − 2) = (4, 2).

To find the vector between two points, work out how far you move right/left and up/down to get from the object to the image.

02

Rotation

10 min

A rotation turns a shape around a fixed point. To fully describe a rotation you need three things: the angle of rotation (e.g. 90°, 180°, 270°), the direction (clockwise or anticlockwise), and the centre of rotation (a coordinate).

To rotate a shape by hand: trace the shape, put your pencil on the centre of rotation, and turn the tracing paper through the given angle in the given direction.

Handy check for rotations about the origin (0, 0): 90° clockwise sends (x, y) → (y, −x); 90° anticlockwise sends (x, y) → (−y, x); 180° sends (x, y) → (−x, −y) — and 180° looks the same clockwise or anticlockwise.

Worked example: rotate point (2, 5) by 180° about the origin → (−2, −5).

03

Reflection

8 min

A reflection flips a shape in a mirror line. Every point on the image is the same perpendicular distance from the mirror line as the matching point on the object, but on the opposite side.

Common mirror lines and the effect on a point (x, y): reflect in the x-axis → (x, −y); reflect in the y-axis → (−x, y); reflect in the line y = x → (y, x); reflect in the line y = −x → (−y, −x).

For a vertical line like x = a or horizontal line like y = b: find the distance from the point to the line, then go the same distance on the other side.

Worked example: reflect (5, 1) in the line x = 2. Distance from x = 2 to x = 5 is 3, so the image is 3 the other side: x = 2 − 3 = −1. Image = (−1, 1).

04

Enlargement: scale factor & centre

10 min

An enlargement is described by a scale factor (SF) and a centre of enlargement. Each point on the image lies on the straight line (ray) from the centre through the matching point on the object, at SF × the distance from the centre.

Ray method (to draw an enlargement): draw a straight line from the centre through each vertex of the object, then mark the image vertex at SF times the distance from the centre along that same ray.

Worked example: a shape has a side of length 4 cm. It is enlarged by scale factor 3. The matching side on the image = 4 × 3 = 12 cm.

Watch out: the area scale factor is the (linear) scale factor squared. Enlarging a shape of area 5 cm² by SF 3 gives an image area of 5 × 3² = 45 cm².

05

Negative & fractional scale factors

9 min

Enlargements aren't always bigger! A fractional scale factor (e.g. SF = ½) produces a smaller image, on the same side of the centre as the object, sitting between the centre and the object.

A negative scale factor (e.g. SF = −2) produces an image on the opposite side of the centre — the shape is turned upside down (rotated 180°) as well as resized. Its size is |SF| times the object.

Worked example: SF = −1. The image is exactly the same size as the object, but on the opposite side of the centre, rotated 180°.

Scale factorSize of imagePosition relative to centre
SF > 1BiggerSame side as object
0 < SF < 1SmallerSame side as object
SF < 0|SF| × object sizeOpposite side, turned 180°

06

Constructions: perpendicular & angle bisectors

11 min

Formal constructions use only a ruler (straight edge) and compasses — no protractor for the construction marks. Always leave your arcs visible; they show your method and earn marks even if the final line is slightly off.

Perpendicular bisector of a line AB: open your compasses to more than half the length of AB. Put the point on A and draw an arc above and below the line; keeping the same radius, put the point on B and draw two more arcs. Join the two points where the arcs cross with a straight line — this line cuts AB at exactly 90°, through its midpoint. Every point on it is equidistant from A and B.

Angle bisector: put the compass point on the vertex and draw an arc that crosses both arms of the angle. Without changing the radius, put the compass point on each of those two crossing points in turn and draw two more arcs that intersect inside the angle. Join the vertex to that intersection point — this line splits the angle exactly in half. Every point on it is equidistant from both arms.

07

Constructions: perpendicular from/to a point

9 min

Perpendicular from a point to a line (the point is not on the line): put the compass point on the given point and draw an arc that crosses the line in two places. Keeping the same (or a wider) radius, put the compass point on each of those two crossing points in turn and draw two arcs that intersect on the other side of the line. Join the original point to that intersection — this line meets the given line at 90° and is the shortest distance from the point to the line.

Perpendicular at a point on a line (the point is on the line): put the compass point on the given point and mark two points on the line, one on each side, the same distance away. Then, using a wider radius, draw intersecting arcs above (or below) the line from each of those two marked points. Join the original point to where the arcs cross — that line is perpendicular to the original line at that point.

All of these constructions rely on the same idea: a point that is the same distance from two other points (found using equal compass radii) lies on the perpendicular bisector of the line joining them.

08

GCSE-style review questions

15 min

1. Point P(3, −1) is translated by the vector (−2, 5). Find the coordinates of the image.

[2 marks]
(3 − 2, −1 + 5) (1)   = (1, 4) (1)

2. A rotation is described using three pieces of information. Name all three.

[3 marks]
Angle of rotation (1)   direction — clockwise or anticlockwise (1)   centre of rotation (1)

3. Point (6, 2) is reflected in the line x = 4. Find the coordinates of the image.

[2 marks]
Distance from x = 4 to x = 6 is 2, so image x = 4 − 2 = 2 (1)   image = (2, 2) (1)

4. A shape is enlarged by scale factor ⅓ from a centre of enlargement. State (a) what happens to the size of the shape, and (b) where the image is positioned relative to the centre.

[2 marks]
(a) The image is smaller than the object (1)   (b) The image is on the same side of the centre as the object, between the centre and the object (1)

5. Explain, using the words "size" and "side", what happens when a shape is enlarged by scale factor −2.

[2 marks]
The image is twice the size of the object (1)   and is on the opposite side of the centre of enlargement, turned 180° (1)

6. Describe the steps to construct the perpendicular bisector of a line segment AB using a ruler and compasses.

[3 marks]
Set compasses to a radius more than half of AB (1)   draw arcs from A and from B (same radius) so they cross above and below the line (1)   join the two crossing points with a straight line (1)

Tip: transformation checklist — Translation needs a vector. Rotation needs angle + direction + centre. Reflection needs a mirror line. Enlargement needs a scale factor + centre (check the sign and whether it's a fraction!). For constructions, never rub out your arcs — they're part of the method marks.
