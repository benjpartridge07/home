Plants, Part 1: Structure & Transpiration

Biology 60 minutes
01

Plant organs & meristem tissue

10 min
  • Root — anchors the plant in the soil, and absorbs water and mineral ions (root hair cells give a large surface area for this)
  • Stem — supports the plant and holds the leaves up towards the light; also carries substances between roots and leaves
  • Leaf — the main site of photosynthesis, and has pores (stomata) for gas exchange and water loss

Meristem tissue is found at the tips of roots and shoots. It is made of unspecialised cells that keep dividing by mitosis, producing new cells throughout the plant's life. These new cells then differentiate into specialised cells — like root hair cells, xylem, or phloem — which is how a plant is able to keep growing.

02

Xylem & phloem: the transport tissues

12 min

Two types of transport tissue run all the way through the root, stem and leaves, forming a continuous network.

Xylem

  • Transports water & dissolved mineral ions
  • One direction only: up, from roots to leaves
  • Made of dead cells joined into hollow tubes, no cytoplasm
  • Walls strengthened with lignin

Phloem

  • Transports dissolved sugars (mainly sucrose)
  • Both directions — up and down the plant
  • Made of living cells
  • Moving sugars this way is called translocation
03

Transpiration: what it is & the pathway

14 min

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from a plant's leaves, mainly through the stomata, by evaporation and diffusion. As water is lost from the top of the plant, more is drawn up from the roots to replace it — this continuous movement is called the transpiration stream.

soil → root hair cell → root → xylem → stem → leaf → evaporates inside leaf → diffuses out through stomata

  • Water is absorbed from the soil by root hair cells, by osmosis
  • It moves across the root and into the xylem
  • It travels up the xylem, through the stem, into the leaves
  • It evaporates from cells inside the leaf into the air spaces, then diffuses out through tiny pores called stomata
  • Each stoma is surrounded by a pair of guard cells, which control whether it is open or closed

04

Factors affecting the rate of transpiration

12 min
FactorIncreases the rate when...Why
Temperaturehigherwater molecules have more energy, so evaporate & diffuse faster
Humiditylowerbigger difference in water vapour concentration inside/outside the leaf, so a steeper gradient for diffusion
Air movement (wind)higherremoves water vapour from around the leaf, keeping the concentration gradient steep
Light intensityhighermore stomata open (for gas exchange during photosynthesis), so more water can escape

A potometer is a piece of apparatus used to measure the rate of water uptake by a cut shoot, as an estimate of its transpiration rate — it works by timing how far an air bubble moves along a capillary tube.

05

GCSE-style review questions

12 min

1. State one function each of the root, the stem, and the leaf.

[3 marks]
Root: anchors the plant / absorbs water and mineral ions (1). Stem: supports the plant / holds leaves up to the light / transports substances (1). Leaf: main site of photosynthesis / gas exchange (1).

2. What is meristem tissue, and why is it important for plant growth?

[3 marks]
Unspecialised cells found at root/shoot tips (1) that divide continuously by mitosis (1); the new cells can differentiate into specialised cells, allowing the plant to keep growing throughout its life (1).

3. Xylem cells are dead, with no cytoplasm, and have walls strengthened with lignin. Explain how this helps xylem carry out its function.

[2 marks]
Being dead/having no cytoplasm leaves a hollow tube, so water can flow through with little resistance (1); lignin strengthens the walls, helping xylem withstand the pressure of water movement and support the plant (1).

4. Describe the pathway water takes from the soil to the air, starting at the root hair cell.

[4 marks]
Absorbed from the soil by root hair cells, by osmosis (1); moves across the root into the xylem (1); travels up the xylem through the stem to the leaves (1); evaporates from cells inside the leaf and diffuses out through the stomata (1).

5. Explain why increasing wind speed around a plant increases its rate of transpiration.

[2 marks]
Wind removes water vapour from around the leaf (1), maintaining a steeper concentration gradient between the inside and outside of the leaf, so diffusion (and evaporation) happens faster (1).

6. A gardener wants to reduce how much water their houseplants lose. Suggest two things they could do, using ideas about the factors affecting transpiration.

[2 marks]
Any two: move to a cooler spot / reduce temperature (1); increase humidity around the plant, e.g. group plants together or mist them (1); move away from draughts/fans, reducing air movement (1); reduce light intensity, e.g. move away from a bright window (1).

Tip: for "explain why the rate increases/decreases" questions, always link back to either evaporation (how fast water turns to vapour) or the concentration gradient of water vapour between the inside and outside of the leaf.
