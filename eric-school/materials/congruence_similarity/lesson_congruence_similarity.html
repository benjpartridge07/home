Congruence & Similarity

Maths 85 minutes Section 05 is Higher tier
01

Congruent or similar?

6 min

Two shapes are congruent if they are exactly the same shape and size. One would fit perfectly on top of the other — even if you had to turn it or flip it over first. All matching sides are equal and all matching angles are equal.

Two shapes are similar if they are the same shape but a different size — one is an enlargement of the other. Matching angles are equal, and every pair of matching sides is in the same ratio (the scale factor).

Quick test: congruent = same angles and same sides. Similar = same angles, sides all multiplied by the same number.

02

The four conditions for congruent triangles

12 min
ConditionStands forWhat must match
SSSSide, Side, SideAll three pairs of sides are equal
SASSide, Angle, SideTwo sides and the angle between them (the included angle)
ASA / AASAngle, Side, AngleTwo angles and a corresponding side
RHSRight angle, Hypotenuse, SideBoth have a right angle, equal hypotenuses, and one other pair of equal sides

Two things that don't prove congruence: AAA (three equal angles only proves the triangles are similar — one could be bigger) and SSA (the angle isn't between the two sides, so two different triangles are possible).

Writing a congruence proof: list three facts, give a reason for each one, then name the condition. Worked example — ABCD is a rectangle; prove triangles ABD and CDB are congruent:

AB = CD (opposite sides of a rectangle are equal). AD = CB (opposite sides of a rectangle are equal). BD is common to both triangles. So the triangles are congruent by SSS.

03

Similar shapes: finding missing lengths

12 min

Step 1 — find a pair of matching sides where you know both lengths. Step 2 — work out the scale factor: SF = new length ÷ original length. Step 3 — multiply (to go bigger) or divide (to go smaller) by the scale factor.

Worked example (diagram, cm): the bases match, 4 and 10, so SF = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. Then x = 3 × 2.5 = 7.5 cm and y = 5 × 2.5 = 12.5 cm.

Going the other way (large → small), divide by 2.5 — or multiply by the reciprocal, 0.4.

Are two shapes similar? Work out the ratio for every pair of matching sides. They're similar only if all the ratios are the same. A 4 × 6 rectangle and a 6 × 8 rectangle are not similar: 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5 but 8 ÷ 6 = 1.33…

04

Similar triangles inside each other

12 min

When a line DE is parallel to BC, triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC. The angle at A is shared, and the parallel lines make corresponding angles equal (angle ADE = angle ABC, angle AED = angle ACB).

The big trap: the side of the large triangle is the whole length AB, not just DB. Always work out AB = AD + DB first.

Worked example (diagram, cm): AD = 3, DB = 6, so AB = 3 + 6 = 9. SF = AB ÷ AD = 9 ÷ 3 = 3. So BC = DE × 3 = 4 × 3 = 12 cm.

Tip: sketch the two triangles separately, side by side, and label the matching sides. It makes it much harder to pair up the wrong lengths.

Doubling every length of a cube makes it 2 wide, 2 deep and 2 tall — so each face has 4 times the area, and the cube holds 8 times the volume. That's why areas scale by k² and volumes by k³. Surface area is an area too, so it also scales by k².

Worked example: two similar cylinders have heights 5 cm and 15 cm. Length SF = 15 ÷ 5 = 3. The small one has volume 40 cm³ and surface area 60 cm². Large volume = 40 × 3³ = 40 × 27 = 1080 cm³. Large surface area = 60 × 3² = 60 × 9 = 540 cm².

Working backwards: if you're given an area or volume ratio, square-root or cube-root it to get back to the length scale factor first.

Worked example: two similar shapes have areas 20 cm² and 45 cm². Area SF = 45 ÷ 20 = 2.25, so length SF = √2.25 = 1.5. If the small one is a solid with volume 16 cm³, the large volume = 16 × 1.5³ = 16 × 3.375 = 54 cm³.

06

GCSE-style review questions

15 min

1. Rectangle P is 5 cm by 8 cm. Rectangle Q is similar to P, and its shorter side is 12.5 cm. Find the longer side of Q.

[2 marks]
SF = 12.5 ÷ 5 = 2.5 (1)   8 × 2.5 = 20 cm (1)

2. Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. M is the midpoint of BC. Prove that triangles ABM and ACM are congruent.

[3 marks]
AB = AC (given / isosceles triangle) and BM = CM (M is the midpoint of BC) (1)   AM is common to both triangles (1)   so congruent by SSS (1)

3. In triangle ABC, D is on AB and E is on AC, with DE parallel to BC. AD = 6 cm, DB = 4 cm and DE = 9 cm. Find BC.

[3 marks]
AB = 6 + 4 = 10 (1)   SF = 10 ÷ 6 = 5/3 (1)   BC = 9 × 5/3 = 15 cm (1)

4. Two similar solids have heights 3 cm and 6 cm. The smaller one has a surface area of 40 cm². Find the surface area of the larger one.

[2 marks]
Length SF = 2, so area SF = 2² = 4 (1)   40 × 4 = 160 cm² (1)

5. Two similar bottles have volumes 250 cm³ and 2000 cm³. The smaller bottle is 12 cm tall. How tall is the larger bottle?

[3 marks]
Volume SF = 2000 ÷ 250 = 8 (1)   length SF = ∛8 = 2 (1)   12 × 2 = 24 cm (1)

6. Two similar shapes have areas 36 cm² and 81 cm². A side on the smaller shape is 10 cm. Find the matching side on the larger shape.

[2 marks]
Area SF = 81 ÷ 36 = 2.25, so length SF = √2.25 = 1.5 (1)   10 × 1.5 = 15 cm (1)

Tip: congruence proofs need three facts, a reason for each, and the condition name at the end. For similar shapes, always find the length scale factor first — then square it for areas, cube it for volumes. Given an area or volume? Square-root or cube-root back to the length scale factor before doing anything else.
