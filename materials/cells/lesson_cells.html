Cells: structure, transport & division

Biology Maths 75 minutes
01

Microscopy & magnification

12 min

Cells are studied using microscopes. An electron microscope has a much higher resolving power than a light microscope, so it can show much finer detail — up to about ×1,000,000 magnification, compared to a useful limit of about ×1,000 for a light microscope.

magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object   or   M = I / R

Image and real size must be in the same units before you divide — watch out for mm, µm (micrometres) and nm (nanometres). Remember: 1 mm = 1000 µm.

Cover the letter you want to find. Cover I → M next to R → I = M × R. Cover M → I over R → M = I / R. Cover R → I over M → R = I / M.

Worked example: a cell has a real width of 0.02 mm. In a photo taken through a microscope it appears 8 mm wide. M = I / R = 8 / 0.02 = ×400.

02

Cell structures

15 min

Animal and plant cells are eukaryotic — their genetic material is enclosed in a nucleus. Bacterial cells are prokaryotic — much smaller, with genetic material as a single DNA loop floating free in the cytoplasm (plus small rings of DNA called plasmids), not enclosed in a nucleus.

03

Transport across membranes

15 min

Substances move into and out of cells in three ways. Cell membranes are partially permeable — they let small molecules like water through, but not larger ones.

ProcessWhat movesDirectionNeeds energy?Example
DiffusionParticles (e.g. gases, dissolved substances)High → low concentrationNoOxygen moving from alveoli into blood
OsmosisWater onlyHigh → low water concentration, through a partially permeable membraneNoWater entering root hair cells from soil
Active transportDissolved substancesLow → high concentration (against the gradient)Yes (from respiration)Mineral ions into root hairs; glucose from gut into blood

Diffusion and osmosis both happen without any energy input — particles just spread out naturally. Active transport is the odd one out: it moves substances the "wrong way" (from low to high concentration), so it needs energy from respiration to make it happen.

04

Cell division: mitosis & meiosis

12 min

A human body cell nucleus contains 46 chromosomes (23 pairs), carrying the DNA and genes. Cells divide by mitosis so organisms can grow and repair damaged tissue. Before dividing, a cell copies all its organelles and replicates its chromosomes; mitosis then splits these into two new cells. You don't need to know the individual stages — just the outcome.

Cells in the reproductive organs divide by meiosis to make gametes (egg and sperm cells). Meiosis divides twice, halving the chromosome number, so each gamete gets a single set of chromosomes — and every gamete produced is genetically different. At fertilisation, two gametes join to restore the full number of chromosomes, and the new cell then grows by mitosis.

05

Cell differentiation & stem cells

8 min

Early in an embryo's development, its cells can grow and divide into any cell type — these are stem cells. As the embryo develops, most cells differentiate: they become specialised to carry out one particular job (e.g. a red blood cell, a nerve cell, a muscle cell).

Once a cell has differentiated, it cannot change into a different kind of cell. However, most adult tissues still contain some stem cells, ready to divide and replace old or damaged cells.

06

GCSE-style review questions

15 min

1. A cell has a real diameter of 0.025 mm. Under a microscope, its image measures 10 mm across. Calculate the magnification used.

[3 marks]
M = I / R (1)   M = 10 / 0.025 (1)   M = ×400 (1)

2. Name two structures found in a plant cell that are not found in an animal cell, and state the function of each.

[4 marks]
Any two of: chloroplasts — site of photosynthesis (2); cell wall — strengthens/supports the cell (2); permanent vacuole — filled with cell sap, keeps the cell rigid (2). [1 mark for naming, 1 for correct function, per structure]

3. Explain why active transport requires energy, but diffusion does not.

[2 marks]
Active transport moves substances from a low to a high concentration, against the concentration gradient (1); diffusion moves substances from high to low concentration, which happens naturally without any energy input (1).

4. A human skin cell divides by mitosis. State the number of chromosomes in each of the two new cells, and explain how they compare genetically to the original cell.

[2 marks]
46 chromosomes in each new cell (1); both new cells are genetically identical to each other and to the original cell (1).

5. Explain why a specialised nerve cell cannot later become a muscle cell, even though both cells originally came from the same stem cell.

[2 marks]
Once a cell has differentiated (become specialised) it cannot change into a different type of cell (1); differentiation is a one-way process, unlike an undifferentiated stem cell which can still become various cell types (1).

Tip: in magnification questions, always check the units of image size and real size match before dividing — converting mm to µm (or back) is the single most common place marks are lost.
