Human body systems: digestion & control

Biology 55 minutes
01

The digestive system

8 min

Digestion breaks down large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble molecules that can pass through the gut wall into the blood. Food travels through a series of organs, each with its own job:

OrganWhat it does
MouthChews food; saliva contains amylase, which starts digesting starch
OesophagusMuscular tube that pushes food down to the stomach
StomachChurns food; produces acid (kills bacteria, gives the right pH for protease) and protease enzymes
Small intestineDigestion is completed here (enzymes from the pancreas and gut wall); digested food is absorbed into the blood
Large intestineAbsorbs water from the remaining material; forms faeces
PancreasProduces amylase, protease & lipase and releases them into the small intestine
LiverProduces bile
02

Enzymes & bile

10 min

Enzymes are biological catalysts — proteins that speed up reactions without being used up themselves. Each enzyme has an active site shaped to fit one particular substrate, like a lock and key.

EnzymeBreaks downInto
AmylaseStarch (a carbohydrate)Sugars
ProteaseProteinAmino acids
LipaseLipids (fats)Fatty acids + glycerol

Bile is made in the liver and stored in the gallbladder before being released into the small intestine. It is not an enzyme — it has two separate jobs:

  • Neutralises the acid that comes from the stomach, giving the alkaline pH that enzymes in the small intestine work best at
  • Emulsifies fats — breaks them into tiny droplets, giving a much larger surface area for lipase to act on
03

The nervous system & reflexes

12 min

The nervous system lets the body respond to a stimulus quickly, using electrical impulses that travel along cells called neurons. Where two neurons meet, there is a tiny gap called a synapse — the impulse triggers the release of a chemical that diffuses across the gap and starts a new impulse in the next neuron.

the reflex arc (continues to an effector, e.g. a muscle)

  • A reflex is a fast, automatic response that does not involve conscious thought
  • The pathway is: stimulus → receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron (in the spinal cord) → motor neuron → effector → response
  • Because the signal is processed in the spinal cord instead of the brain, reflexes are much faster than a considered response — useful for protecting the body from harm (e.g. pulling a hand away from something hot)
04

The endocrine system

10 min

Hormones are chemical messengers, released directly into the blood by glands. They travel to a target organ, where they produce an effect. Compared with the nervous system, hormonal control is slower to start but produces effects that last longer.

GlandHormoneEffect
Pituitary glandSeveralThe "master gland" — releases hormones that control other glands
PancreasInsulinReduces blood glucose concentration
Adrenal glandsAdrenalinePrepares the body for "fight or flight" (raises heart rate, etc.)
Thyroid glandThyroxineRegulates metabolic rate
Ovaries / testesOestrogen / testosteroneReproduction and secondary sexual characteristics

Nervous vs hormonal control — the one thing to remember: nervous signals are electrical and travel along neurons, fast but short-lived; hormonal signals are chemical and travel in the blood, slower but longer-lasting.

05

GCSE-style review questions

9 min

1. What is an enzyme?

[2 marks]
A biological catalyst (1); made of protein, it speeds up a reaction without being used up (1).

2. Name the enzyme that breaks down protein, and state what it is broken down into.

[2 marks]
Protease (1); broken down into amino acids (1).

3. Explain the role of bile in digestion.

[3 marks]
Produced in the liver (1); neutralises stomach acid, giving the right (alkaline) pH for enzymes in the small intestine (1); emulsifies fats into tiny droplets, increasing the surface area for lipase to act on (1).

4. Describe the pathway of a reflex arc, from stimulus to response.

[4 marks]
Stimulus detected by a receptor (1); impulse passes along a sensory neuron to the CNS/spinal cord (1); passes across a synapse to a relay neuron then a motor neuron (1); impulse travels to an effector, which produces the response (1).

5. Explain why reflexes are faster than responses that involve conscious thought.

[2 marks]
The impulse is processed by a relay neuron in the spinal cord (1), rather than travelling all the way to the brain and back, so it doesn't involve conscious thought/decision-making (1).

6. Compare hormonal and nervous communication in terms of speed and how long the effect lasts.

[2 marks]
Nervous communication is fast-acting but short-lived (1); hormonal communication is slower-acting but longer-lasting (1).

Tip: if a question mentions "master gland," that's the pituitary gland — it doesn't cause an effect directly, it releases hormones that tell other glands what to do.
