Percentages: Change, Interest & Financial Maths

Maths 55 minutes
01

The multiplier method

12 min

The fast way to increase or decrease by a percentage is to convert the change into a single decimal multiplier, then multiply once.

  • Increase by 15%: the new amount is 100% + 15% = 115% of the original → multiplier 1.15
  • Decrease by 20%: the new amount is 100% − 20% = 80% of the original → multiplier 0.80

Worked examples: increase £60 by 15% → 60 × 1.15 = £69.   Decrease £150 by 20% → 150 × 0.8 = £120.

02

Reverse percentages

12 min

Sometimes you're given the amount after a percentage change, and asked to find the original (100%) amount. The trap: don't just add or subtract the percentage from the final amount — instead, divide by the multiplier.

Worked example: a jacket costs £69 after a 15% price increase. Find the original price.

multiplier = 1.15  →  original = 69 ÷ 1.15 = £60

Another: a sale price is £120 after a 20% decrease. Find the original price.

multiplier = 0.80  →  original = 120 ÷ 0.8 = £150

03

Simple interest

10 min

Simple interest adds the same amount every year, always calculated from the original amount (the principal).

I = P × R × T ÷ 100

P — principal (starting amount)  ·  R — rate, as a %  ·  T — time, in years

Worked example: £500 at 4% simple interest for 3 years.

I = 500 × 4 × 3 ÷ 100 = £60  →  total after 3 years = £560

04

Compound interest

12 min

Compound interest pays interest on the interest already earned, not just on the original amount — so it grows faster the longer it runs. Use the multiplier, raised to the power of the number of years.

Amount = P × (multiplier)T

Worked example: £500 at 4% compound interest for 3 years.

Amount = 500 × 1.043 = 500 × 1.124864 = £562.43 (2 d.p.)

05

GCSE-style review questions

13 min

1. Write the multiplier for a 12% increase.

[1 mark]
1.12 (1)

2. A shop increases a £40 item by 25%. Find the new price.

[2 marks]
40 × 1.25 (1)   = £50 (1)

3. After a 30% decrease, a laptop costs £280. Find the original price.

[2 marks]
280 ÷ 0.7 (1)   = £400 (1)

4. Calculate the simple interest earned on £600 at 3% per year for 4 years.

[3 marks]
I = 600 × 3 × 4 ÷ 100 (1)   = 7200 ÷ 100 (1)   = £72 (1)

5. £1000 is invested at 2% compound interest for 3 years. Calculate the total amount after 3 years, to the nearest penny.

[3 marks]
Amount = 1000 × 1.023 (1)   = 1000 × 1.061208 (1)   = £1061.21 (1)

Tip: before doing anything, decide what 100% represents. For "increase/decrease by X%" questions, 100% is the original amount. For reverse percentage questions, the amount you've been given is not 100% — it's the multiplier percentage, so you divide by the multiplier to get back to 100%.
