Density + Rearranging equations

Physics Maths 60 minutes
01

What density actually measures

10 min

Density is how much mass is packed into a given amount of space. A brick and a sponge can be the same size (same volume) but the brick has far more mass squeezed into it — so the brick is denser.

The equation: density = mass ÷ volume, or in symbols: ρ = m / V

  • ρ (rho) — density, usually in kg/m³ or g/cm³
  • m — mass, in kg or g
  • V — volume, in m³ or cm³

This is already confident territory — the real point of today is using this familiar equation as safe ground to build the skill of rearranging, which then transfers to every other equation-based question in the course.

02

Rearranging: the proper way, and the shortcut

15 min

The proper way (algebra): whatever you do to one side of an equation, you must do to the other. Starting from ρ = m / V, to get m on its own, multiply both sides by V:

ρ × V = (m / V) × V  →  m = ρV

To get V on its own instead, start again from ρ = m / V. Multiply both sides by V (giving ρV = m), then divide both sides by ρ:

V = m / ρ

This "do the same to both sides" method works on every equation in the course, not just this one — that's why it's worth practicing properly rather than just memorising three versions of the density formula.

The shortcut (formula triangle): cover up the letter you want to find — the positions of the remaining two letters tell you the operation. Cover m and you see ρ next to V (multiply). Cover ρ and you see m over V (divide). Cover V and you see m over ρ (divide).

Useful for speed in an exam, but it only works for this exact shape of equation — the algebra method above is what actually transfers to harder equations later in the course, so aim to understand both.

03

Now the other way round

10 min

The exam won't always ask for density. Sometimes you're given density and one other quantity, and asked to find mass or volume — that's when rearranging becomes essential rather than optional.

Worked example: A block has a density of 8 g/cm³ and a volume of 5 cm³. Find its mass.

m = ρV  →  m = 8 × 5  →  m = 40 g

04

GCSE-style review questions

15 min

1. Write down the equation that links density, mass and volume.

[1 mark]
density = mass ÷ volume, or ρ = m / V (1)

2. A metal block has a mass of 270 g and a volume of 30 cm³. Calculate its density.

[2 marks]
ρ = m ÷ V = 270 ÷ 30 (1)   ρ = 9 g/cm³ (1)

3. A liquid has a density of 1.2 g/cm³. Calculate the mass of 50 cm³ of the liquid. Show how you rearranged the equation.

[3 marks]
m = ρV (1)   m = 1.2 × 50 (1)   m = 60 g (1)

4. A block of wood has a density of 0.6 g/cm³ and a mass of 90 g. Calculate its volume. Show how you rearranged the equation.

[3 marks]
V = m ÷ ρ (1)   V = 90 ÷ 0.6 (1)   V = 150 cm³ (1)

5. Two blocks have the same mass, but block A has a larger volume than block B. Which block has the higher density? Explain your answer.

[2 marks]
Block B has the higher density (1), because the same mass is packed into a smaller volume, and density = mass ÷ volume increases as volume decreases for a fixed mass (1).

Tip: on the calculation questions, always write the rearranged equation as its own line before substituting numbers in — that's a guaranteed method mark even on a day the arithmetic goes wrong.
