Speed, distance & acceleration

Physics Maths 60 minutes
01

Scalars and vectors

10 min

Some quantities need a direction to be complete, and some don't. A quantity with no direction is called a scalar; a quantity that includes a direction is called a vector.

QuantityTypeMeaning
DistanceScalarHow far something moves — no direction needed
DisplacementVectorStraight-line distance from start to finish, plus direction
SpeedScalarHow fast something moves — no direction needed
VelocityVectorSpeed in a given direction

Typical speeds worth memorising: walking 1.5 m/s, running 3 m/s, cycling 6 m/s, sound in air 330 m/s.

02

Distance, speed and time

15 min

distance = speed × time   or   s = vt

s — distance, in metres, m  ·  v — speed, in metres per second, m/s  ·  t — time, in seconds, s

Cover the letter you want to find. Top-over-bottom means divide, side-by-side on the bottom means multiply.

Cover s → v next to t → s = vt. Cover v → s over t → v = s / t. Cover t → s over v → t = s / v.

The gradient (steepness) of a distance–time graph equals the speed. A steeper line means faster speed. A flat, horizontal line means the object has stopped.

03

Acceleration

15 min

acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time   or   a = Δv / t

a — acceleration, in metres per second squared, m/s²  ·  Δv — change in velocity, in m/s  ·  t — time, in s

A negative acceleration means the object is decelerating (slowing down). Near the Earth's surface, an object in free fall accelerates at about 9.8 m/s².

The gradient of a velocity–time graph equals the acceleration. The area underneath the graph equals the distance travelled.

04

GCSE-style review questions

15 min

1. State whether distance and velocity are each a scalar or a vector quantity, and explain the difference between the two terms "scalar" and "vector".

[3 marks]
Distance is a scalar (1). Velocity is a vector (1). A vector quantity has a direction as well as a size; a scalar quantity has no direction (1).

2. A train travels 300 m in 15 s at a constant speed. Calculate its speed.

[3 marks]
v = s / t (1)   v = 300 / 15 (1)   v = 20 m/s (1)

3. A skater speeds up from 2 m/s to 14 m/s in 6 s. Calculate their acceleration.

[3 marks]
Δv = 14 − 2 = 12 m/s (1)   a = Δv / t = 12 / 6 (1)   a = 2 m/s² (1)

4. Explain what a horizontal section of a distance–time graph tells you about an object's motion.

[2 marks]
The object is stationary / at rest (1), because the distance is not changing over time, so the speed (gradient) is zero (1).

5. Explain what the area under a velocity–time graph represents.

[1 mark]
The distance travelled by the object (1).

Tip: whenever a question gives you two velocities instead of "Δv" directly (like question 3), your first step is always to subtract them — that subtraction is worth its own method mark, separate from using the equation itself.
