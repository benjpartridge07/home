Kick-off lesson · Session 8, Week 5

Cell specialisation

Biology 55 minutes
01

Structure fits function

6 min

You already know that stem cells differentiate into specialised cells, each built to do one job well. The big exam skill here isn't memorising a list — it's being able to look at an unfamiliar cell's structure and explain why that structure helps it do its job.

The method for every "explain how structure relates to function" question is the same three steps:

  1. Name the structural feature.
  2. Say what that feature physically does (longer/more/thinner/etc.).
  3. Link it to the cell's job.
02

Specialised animal cells

14 min

Sperm cell

  • Long tail — swims to reach the egg
  • Many mitochondria in the middle — energy for swimming
  • Acrosome (enzyme tip) — digests through the egg's membrane

Nerve cell

  • Long axon — carries signals over long distances
  • Branched endings — connect to many other cells
  • Insulating layer around axon — speeds up the signal

Muscle cell

  • Long, thin fibre that can contract — shortens to cause movement
  • Many mitochondria — energy for contraction
03

Specialised plant cells

14 min

Root hair cell

  • Long hair-like extension — large surface area for absorbing water & minerals from soil
  • No chloroplasts — underground, no light

Xylem cell

  • Hollow tube, no cell contents — water flows through easily
  • Thick, lignin-strengthened walls — support & withstand pressure

Phloem cell

  • Sieve tube with perforated end plates — sugars flow through
  • Companion cell alongside — provides energy for active transport of sugars

04

Applying it to an unfamiliar cell

10 min

Exam questions often describe a cell you haven't specifically studied and ask you to explain its structure. Use the same 3-step method — it works even when you don't recognise the cell.

Worked example: "A cell lining the small intestine has many finger-like projections on one surface. Suggest how this helps the cell absorb nutrients."

1. Feature: finger-like projections.
2. What it does: greatly increases the surface area.
3. Link: a bigger surface area means more nutrients can be absorbed at once.

05

GCSE-style review questions

13 min

1. Explain how the structure of a sperm cell is adapted to its function.

[3 marks]
It has a long tail, which lets it swim to the egg (1); it has many mitochondria, which release energy for swimming (1); it has an acrosome containing enzymes, which digests through the egg's membrane (1).

2. Explain how the structure of a xylem cell is adapted to its function.

[3 marks]
It is a hollow tube with no cell contents, so water can flow through easily (1); its walls are thickened with lignin, making it strong (1); this lets it support the plant as well as transport water (1).

3. State two ways a root hair cell is adapted for absorbing water and mineral ions.

[2 marks]
It has a long hair-like extension, which gives it a large surface area (1); this means more water/mineral ions can be absorbed (1).

4. A muscle cell contains far more mitochondria than most other cells. Suggest why.

[2 marks]
Muscle cells need a lot of energy to contract (1); mitochondria are the site of respiration, which releases this energy (1).

5. A gland cell that produces digestive enzymes contains an unusually large number of ribosomes. Suggest why.

[2 marks]
Ribosomes are where proteins are made (1); enzymes are proteins, so more ribosomes let the cell produce enzymes faster/in larger quantities (1).

Tip: "suggest" questions (like Q4 and Q5) describe a cell you haven't necessarily studied by name — don't panic. Use the 3-step method from Section 04 and reason it through; the marks are for the logic, not for recognising the cell.
