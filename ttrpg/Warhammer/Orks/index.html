Ork Battle Card

Eric printed a Battlewagon, so the Nobz get a ride instead of footslogging — the whole plan below is built around minimising time spent walking through T’au firing lanes and maximising the turn you're already swinging in melee.

Why the Nobz ride

Nobz are your best damage-per-model, but they're also what T’au's alpha-strike shooting deletes fastest if they walk. The Battlewagon keeps them safe until they're close enough to matter, then delivers them into charge range on turn 2.

02

Turn Plan

Turn 1

  • Gretchin screen forward — eat overwatch, block Kroot/deep strike pockets, sit an objective
  • Battlewagon (Nobz aboard) and both Boyz mobs advance using the wagon and terrain as cover
  • War Trak works a flank, staying out of LOS where possible — threaten a backfield objective or a light vehicle
  • Hold Da Jump unless the board clearly rewards an early charge threat

Turn 2

  • Battlewagon closes in, rams with Deff Rolla into whatever it's adjacent to for free damage
  • Nobz + Bannernob disembark and charge — target their biggest shooting threat, not screening chaff
  • Boyz mobs keep closing under cover; Warboss/Bigboss keep them stubborn
  • Big Mek + Gretchin hold the home objective

Turn 3+

  • Stay in combat — T’au fold in melee, so consolidate into new targets each turn so they can't disengage and reset their range advantage
  • Call the Waaagh! on the turn your big multi-unit charge lands, so Boyz and Nobz swing together
  • Use the War Trak to pick off characters or light vehicles falling back from combat
03

Kill Priority

  1. Markerlight / drone controller units — multiply everything else's accuracy, remove first if reachable
  2. Riptide / Broadsides — biggest single damage source, but bad in melee — a perfect Nobz target
  3. Crisis suits — mobile flamers/plasma, kill or force them off objectives
  4. Kroot / screens — cheap for Orks to trade Gretchin into, don't waste Nobz here

The read

T’au want distance and clean firing lanes. Every choice on this card is about removing both — minimise turns spent in the open under fire, and maximise the turn you're already swinging.
